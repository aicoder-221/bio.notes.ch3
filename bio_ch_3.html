🧬 Reproductive Health — NCERT Notes

CLASS XII · BIOLOGY
🌸

Definition of Reproductive Health

▼
📖 DefinitionA state of physical, emotional, and behavioral well-being in all matters related to the reproductive system.
Important NOT just absence of disease!
It includes:
✅ Healthy reproductive organs ✅ Safe & satisfying sex life ✅ Freedom from STIs ✅ Ability to reproduce if desired ✅ Knowledge of contraception ✅ Emotional maturity
❓

Government Initiatives

▼
⭐
India = FIRST country in the world to start a national family planning programme (1951) — extremely high-yield exam fact!
  • Family Planning Programme (1951) — contraceptive awareness, sterilization, spacing methods
  • RCH Programme — safe pregnancy, delivery, immunization, STI control, contraceptive availability
  • Sex education in schools — healthy attitudes, STI awareness, avoiding teen pregnancy, busting myths
  • Awareness campaigns — mass media, posters, songs, demonstrations, counselling by govt + NGOs
  • Hospitals & clinics upgraded — MCH care, prenatal & postnatal care, safe deliveries
📊

Outcomes of Reproductive Health Awareness

▼
↓ Infant mortality ↓ Maternal mortality ↓ STI spread ↓ Sexual assault ↓ Unwanted pregnancy ↑ Quality of life
📈

Population Explosion

▼
📖 DefinitionRapid & uncontrolled rise in human population in a short time.

📊 NCERT Data

  • At independence (1947) → ~350 million
  • By year 2000 → crossed 1 billion (100 crores)

⚠️ Causes

  • ↓ Death rate
  • ↓ Maternal mortality rate
  • ↓ Infant mortality rate
  • Better healthcare, vaccines, nutrition
  • Birth rate did NOT decrease proportionally
⚖️

Population Stabilization

▼
📖 DefinitionAchieving a level where birth rate ≈ death rate, so population stops rising sharply.
Government Measures
  • Family Planning Programme (1951) — 1st country globally; contraception, birth spacing, small-family norms
  • Awareness & Contraception — TV, radio, posters, health workers; promote small family & delayed marriage
  • Incentives — monetary rewards for adopting small family norms
  • RCH Programme — mother & child health, safe deliveries, contraceptive availability
  • Delayed Marriage — shorter reproductive life → fewer children
  • Female Education — educated women plan families, understand contraception, avoid early pregnancy
✅ Outcome: Growth rate is slowing; India is moving toward population stabilization.
📖

Definition & Ideal Contraceptive

▼
📖 DefinitionMethods to prevent pregnancy by preventing gamete release / fertilization / implantation.
Ideal Contraceptive Should Be:
User-friendly Easily available Reversible No side effects Not affect sexual drive
🌿

A. Natural Methods

▼

No drugs, no devices. Prevent chance meeting of sperm & ovum.

MethodMechanismNote
Periodic AbstinenceAvoid sex during fertile window (days 10–17)Ovulation avoidance
Withdrawal / Coitus InterruptusMale withdraws before ejaculationHigh failure risk
Lactational AmenorrheaIntense breastfeeding → prolactin → no ovulationOnly up to 6 months postpartum
🛡️

B. Barrier Methods

▼

Physically prevent sperm from reaching ovum.

  • Condoms — male (penis) or female (vagina); prevent pregnancy + STIs; disposable
  • Diaphragm / Cervical cap / Vaults — rubber devices inserted into female tract; block cervix; reusable
⭐
Only condoms provide dual protection — contraception + STI prevention.
🌀

C. IUDs (Intrauterine Devices)

▼
⭐
IUDs are the most widely used contraceptive method in India.
TypeExamplesMechanism
Non-medicatedLippes LoopPhysical barrier in uterus
Copper-releasingCu-T, Cu-7, Multiload 375Release Cu²⁺ → ↓ sperm motility, ↓ fertilizing capacity
Hormone-releasingProgestasert, LNG-20Thicken cervical mucus + inhibit ovulation + inhibit implantation
💊

D. Oral Contraceptives (Pills)

▼
TypeCompositionMechanism / Use
Combined pillsEstrogen + ProgesteroneInhibit ovulation, alter cervical mucus; taken 21 days + 7-day break
Mini pillsOnly progesteroneThicken mucus, inhibit implantation
Emergency contraceptivesHigh-dose hormonesWithin 72 hours of unprotected sex; prevent ovulation + implantation
💉

E. Injectables & Implants

▼
  • Injectables — intramuscular; long-acting; contain progesterone ± estrogen
  • Implants — placed under skin on arm
  • Mechanism — prevent ovulation + thicken cervical mucus + inhibit implantation
✂️

F. Surgical Methods (Sterilization)

▼

♂ Vasectomy (Male)

  • Small cut in scrotum
  • Vas deferens cut + tied
  • No sperm in ejaculate
  • Does NOT affect hormones or sex drive

♀ Tubectomy (Female)

  • Fallopian tubes cut & tied
  • Prevents ovum from reaching uterus
  • Prevents sperm from reaching ovum
Both are permanent methods of contraception.
✅

Benefits & Common Misconceptions

▼
Controls population growth Reduces unwanted pregnancies Reduces abortions Helps birth spacing Prevents STIs (condoms)
Misconceptions Clarified (NCERT)
  • Condoms do NOT significantly reduce sexual pleasure
  • Oral pills do NOT cause infertility if used properly
  • IUDs do NOT interfere with intercourse
  • Breastfeeding works only for 6 months, not longer
⚖️

Medical Termination of Pregnancy (MTP)

▼
📖 DefinitionIntentional termination of pregnancy using medical or surgical methods.
Reasons for MTP
  • Unwanted pregnancy (contraceptive failure, unplanned, or rape)
  • Risk to mother's health / life-threatening conditions
  • Foetal abnormalities — congenital defects, genetic disorders
Legal Status
  • Legal in India under the MTP Act, 1971
  • Permissible up to 20 weeks (NCERT text figure)
  • Only by qualified & registered practitioners
  • Only in approved hospitals / clinics
Safety Timeline

✅ 1st Trimester

Safer — recommended timing for MTP

⚠️ 2nd Trimester

Riskier — complications higher

🚨

Female Foeticide & Misuse of MTP

▼

High Yield Misuse of amniocentesis + ultrasound to determine sex → sex-selective abortions → female foeticide → declining sex ratio in India.

⚖️
PCPNDT Act (1994) — Pre-Conception & Pre-Natal Diagnostic Techniques Act — bans sex determination before birth.
Complications of Unsafe MTP
Bleeding Infection Infertility Death

Transmission, Symptoms & Complications

▼
Modes of Transmission
Sexual intercourse Shared needles Blood transfusion Mother to child (birth/pregnancy)
⚠️
"Early symptoms are often absent or easily overlooked" — key NCERT line. People don't realize they're infected.
When Symptoms Appear:
Itching Fluid discharge Slight pain Swelling Irritation in genital area
Complications if Untreated
  • Gonorrhoea & Syphilis → PID, infertility, stillbirths, organ damage
  • HIV → AIDS — life-threatening immune deficiency
💊

Prevention & Treatment

▼

Prevention

  • Avoid sex with multiple partners
  • Use condoms
  • Early detection / consult doctors
  • Safe blood transfusions
  • Avoid sharing needles

Treatment

  • Bacterial / Fungal / Protozoan → Curable
  • Viral (HIV, Herpes, HPV) → No cure, only management
IMP Viral infections cannot be cured completely.
🏫
Adolescents are high-risk (lack of awareness + peer pressure). NCERT emphasizes sex education in schools.

IVF, ZIFT & IUT

▼

IVF — In Vitro Fertilization ("Test Tube Baby")

  • Ova collected from woman + sperm from man
  • Fertilization OUTSIDE body (in glass dish/test tube)
  • Forms zygote → then transferred
TechniqueWhat is TransferredWhereStage
ZIFT — Zygote Intrafallopian TransferZygoteFallopian tubeUp to 8-blastomere stage
IUT — Intra-Uterine TransferEmbryoUterusMore than 8-blastomere stage
⭐
ZIFT = zygote to fallopian tube; IUT = embryo (>8 cells) to uterus. Classic exam distinction!
💉

ICSI — Intracytoplasmic Sperm Injection

▼
  • Used when: sperm count very low OR sperm can't penetrate ovum
  • A single sperm injected directly into the ovum
  • Embryo formed → transferred to uterus
🎯 Key phrase: "single sperm injected directly into cytoplasm of ovum"
🎁

GIFT — Gamete Intrafallopian Transfer

▼
  • Ova + sperms placed together into woman's fallopian tube
  • Fertilization happens inside the fallopian tube (not outside)
  • Requires normal functional fallopian tubes in woman
⭐
GIFT ≠ IVF. In GIFT, gametes are transferred (not embryo). Fertilization is in vivo (inside body).
🏥

AI, IUI & Surrogacy

▼
TechniqueProcessUsed When
AI — Artificial InseminationSemen introduced into vagina or uterusMale cannot deposit semen / low sperm count
IUI — Intrauterine InseminationProcessed semen (↑ motility) placed directly in uterusAdvanced form of AI
SurrogacyIntended couple's embryo placed in surrogate mother's uterus; she carries to termWoman cannot maintain pregnancy / repeated miscarriages / absent uterus
